## Notes for testing

**Published BPM for the beat detector.** These seven tempos were published and confirmed against the audio: Windswept 79, Speedier than Photons 94, Devastation and Revenge 114, Notanico Merengue 120, Drama 130, Opportunity Walks 156 and Ripped Apart 174.
- Drama also shows a strong 87 BPM (2/3) periodicity, which makes it a good test for octave and triplet errors.
- Ripped Apart shows the same at 87 BPM (half-time).

**Catalog BPMs that are not safe as ground truth:**
- *Furious Freak*: incompetech lists 160, but every section of the audio analyses at about 128 BPM. Treat the listed value as unreliable.
- *Hall of the Mountain King*: the piece speeds up throughout (accelerando), so the listed 160 is at best the final tempo.

**Calm and loud sections** (5-second RMS loudness profile; EBU R128 loudness range in LU):
- *Furious Freak*: quiet intro at about -33 dBFS, explodes at about 0:30–0:44 to about -13, drops out again around 1:50–2:00, then is loud to the end. LRA 18.8 LU.
- *Devastation and Revenge*: low hum at about -33 dBFS until about 0:49, then full orchestra and drums at about -20; climax around 2:01, quiet ending from about 2:35. LRA 18.2 LU.
- *Hall of the Mountain King*: starts quiet at about -29 and builds steadily to about -13 from roughly 1:30. LRA 17.6 LU.
- For contrast, the metal, disco and techno tracks are flat-out loud (LRA about 1–2 LU).

**Tempo change:**
- *Invariance*: the composer describes it as "one file that contains the same piece in 2 tempos; slower and faster". The rhythmic periodicity in the audio clearly changes at roughly 1:50–2:05. The BPM of each half is not published: the catalog gives only 108, and the rough analysis suggests about 100–103 before the change and something faster (about 154?) after, but that is not confirmed.
- *Hall of the Mountain King*: a gradual accelerando rather than a step change.
- Also smaller rubato: *Windswept* "slows down in key moments".

**Other notes:**
- Ripped Apart and Drama were 35–43 MB WAVs on OpenGameArt. They were converted here to 192 kbps MP3 with ffmpeg. All other files are the original MP3s, unmodified.
- Hall of the Mountain King: Grieg's composition (1876) is public domain, and incompetech says the source recordings were placed in the public domain by the MusOpen project. Kevin MacLeod's mix is distributed under CC BY 4.0.

## Original power-metal tracks (generated)

These three tracks are original compositions in the extreme power metal style: fast double-kick and blast beats, galloping riffs, twin harmony leads and long guitar solos. Every sound is synthesized from scratch by `songgen/` (Python with numpy and scipy). The guitars, bass, drums, synth leads, pads and bells all come from oscillators and noise. There are no samples, no third-party audio and no borrowed melodies.

To re-render after editing the songs:

```
cd songgen && .venv/bin/python songs.py all
```

Use `1`, `2` or `3` to render a single song.

Each song keeps a constant, exact tempo, so these are the most reliable beat-detector ground truth in the folder. Bar 1 starts at 0:00, so beats fall exactly on multiples of 60/BPM. The files end with about 4 seconds of reverb tail and fade.

- **Skyforge Inferno**: E minor, 200 BPM.
  - Calm sections: bell intro 0:00–0:09.6 and breakdown 1:36.0–1:45.6.
  - Solo block 1:45.6–2:33.6: melodic, then shred runs, then keyboard solo (2:04.8), then sweeps and tapping (2:14.4), then twin lead.
  - Key change up 2 semitones at 2:48.0.
- **Starfall Overdrive**: F# minor, 184 BPM.
  - Calm sections: synth-arpeggio intro 0:00–0:10.4 and breakdown 1:39.1–1:49.6.
  - Solo block 1:49.6–2:31.3, including guitar-vs-keyboard trades at 2:00.0 and sweeps and tapping at 2:10.4.
  - The 3+3+2 syncopated theme fools a naive detector into hearing about 122 BPM (2/3 of the real tempo). That makes it a good test case.
- **Wyvern Reactor**: C# minor, 216 BPM.
  - Starts at full speed with no calm intro.
  - Calm interlude 1:04.4–1:13.3.
  - 48-bar solo block 1:13.3–2:06.7.
  - Key change up 1 semitone at 2:15.6.
  - A naive detector hears about 72 BPM (1/3 of the real tempo).
